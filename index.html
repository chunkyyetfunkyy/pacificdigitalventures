VocaTranslateHIPAA-compliant medical translation for iPad

Real-time, two-way spoken translation built for the exam room. Speech recognition tuned for orthopedic, surgical and general medical vocabulary, with a dual-sided layout so patient and provider can talk face to face across one device.

  • 46 languages, with terminology weighting for medical conversations.
  • Smart Hold Mode: patient and provider buttons with 180° text rotation for across-the-table use.
  • Compliance by design: BAAs with cloud providers, AES-256 encryption, zero PHI retained after a session, 365-day audit logging.
  • Enterprise-ready: organization-managed accounts, first-login password reset, lockout after failed attempts, 15-minute inactivity timeout.

How we work

Small team. Clinical instincts. No shortcuts on compliance.

01

Built by a clinician

Our founder still sees patients. Every feature is tested against the question "would I use this at 4:45pm on a Friday with a full waiting room?"

02

Compliance from day one

We sign BAAs before writing code, encrypt everything, keep audit trails, and store no more patient data than the job requires. Usually none.

03

Native where it matters

Our clinical apps are written in Swift for iOS and iPadOS, so they feel like the rest of the device and inherit Apple's security, accessibility and device-management features.

About

Why a PA started a software company.

Most healthcare software is built far away from the people who use it. The result is tools that technically work and practically get in the way. Pacific Digital Ventures is the opposite bet: a small company where the person writing the code is also the person using it on shift.

That's why VocaTranslate was tuned for orthopedic and surgical vocabulary, why Master Scheduler counts shift deficits the way a real schedule-maker does, and why our intake flow asks about work injuries and auto claims up front instead of on page nine.

It's also why we take compliance seriously. Clinicians live with the consequences of a privacy failure. We build as if we'll be the ones explaining it to the patient, because we might be.
