# Judging — three lenses

## judge:craft

**Verdict:** NOW PLAYING: ROOM 2 wins, narrowly, on the lens I was given. Backlit is the more finished system and the safer brand, and on pure craft it edges Room 2, but its vocabulary (hof vinyl, condensed uppercase, a marquee ticker) could front any Korean bar in any city, whereas Room 2's three load-bearing ideas, the sung-lyric fill, the song-book menu and the wall-phone booking CTA, only make sense for a place whose reason to have a website is renting the back rooms, and they are built from facts (F#119, F#120, F#3) rather than from a mood. Its Hangul is honest and it is the only deck that treats the colloquial spelling as a question for the owner rather than a decoration. The win is conditional: strip the scanlines, the Screen Blue glow and the flip-to-2AM digits (retro-CRT defaults), replace the three-up quote grid with Backlit's offset stuck-label column, make the nav queue advance only on user action, and graft Backlit's hero hierarchy so 오리궁뎅이 is lit and read before DUCK BUTT, then add Anju Board's 'What the regulars order' row and the romanization-plus-gloss rule. Built that way, it is the only one of the three that a jury would recognise as this bar and no other.

### Backlit: the lit box on Kawaiahaʻo — D8 F9 P8 B7 = 32

The most coherent system of the three: one object (a backlit hof sign box in flat red, green and vinyl) generates the hero, the section headers, the flavor panels and the OPEN chip, so the page has a single material logic instead of a collection of effects. The Korean is honest and functional, not costume: 안주, 소주, 노래방 and 영업중 are exactly the words a Korean bar hangs on vinyl, Black Han Sans is the right register for them, and the no-letterspacing and no-body-copy rules show someone who has actually looked at Hangul set badly. Fit is the strongest here because it frames the thing the press keeps repeating (F#162, F#165: gray, nearly windowless, 'sketchy as ever') as the point rather than hiding it, and the 2010 'strange bird' and supplier-giggle stories (F#114, F#150) land on the first scroll. Craft demerits: Anton-uppercase-condensed is now a very common 'bold' default and the address ticker (motion 3) is the one element that smells like a template; the 'sticker press' hover is stock. The sign box also owes nothing to THIS bar specifically; it could front any Korean hof, and the direction admits the real sign has a comic duck it has never seen. Buildability is sound (every state is a finished document without JS, red floored at 40px via a custom property) but four families including Archivo with a width axis plus a schematic map strip is the heaviest spec.

- Biggest risk: Generic Korea rather than specific Kakaʻako: the hof vinyl box is a mainland-Seoul image, and if the owner's real sign turns out to be a cartoon duck on a different palette, the whole system is a costume for someone else's building. Secondary: the ballast warm-up on slow cell service reading as a dead page.
- Must graft: The hero reading order: 오리궁둥이 lit on a red rail ABOVE and BEFORE 'DUCK BUTT', so the owner reads his bar's name in Korean first, plus the 영업중 OPEN chip as the hours device.

### NOW PLAYING: ROOM 2 — D9 F8 P9 B6 = 32

This is the only direction whose core ideas are impossible anywhere but a karaoke bar: the lyric sweep (white filling to cue-yellow left to right, exactly what a TJ/Kumyoung machine does), the menu as a song book with four-digit codes, the wall phone from F#119 as the booking CTA with the real number on the handset, and the 5–8 / 8–11 block chips from F#120. Those are typographic and motion ideas with a reason to exist, and they put the site's actual business function (book a room) at the centre of the world instead of in a footer ticket. Hangul is used correctly and sparingly, always paired on the same line, and the direction is the only one honest enough to flag that 궁뎅이 is a colloquial spelling that needs the owner's say-so. Deductions for craft: the scanline overlay, the Screen Blue outer glow and the 'flip to 2 AM' digit roll are retro-CRT defaults that an awards jury has seen a thousand times, the three-up 'TV wall' of quotes on desktop is precisely the equal-card grid the brief warns against, and an in-page nav that advances itself every four seconds is an accessibility complaint waiting to happen. The hero also undersells its own best asset by setting 오리궁뎅이 in Smoke. Buildability is the weakest of the three: the two-tone background-clip text with an animated split, an auto-rotating nav and a mostly black page with only two bright panels need real discipline at 50% brightness in a bar.

- Biggest risk: Pastiche. One wrong move (a score, applause, a fake remote) and the room becomes a theme-park ride; the direction knows this and writes a rule, but the scanlines and glow are already halfway there. Spelling of 궁뎅이 is the second risk: get it wrong and the first thing a Korean reads is a typo.
- Must graft: The lyric sweep as the hero motion: DUCK BUTT fills Lyric White to Cue Yellow left to right as if sung, with the reduced-motion split state (DUCK yellow, BUTT white). Second choice if the sweep cannot be carried: the wall phone as the booking CTA.

### Anju Board — D5 F6 P5 B8 = 24

The best-reasoned Hangul treatment (three words, each with romanization and a one-line gloss, which is the right move for a room where locals order tacos and Koreans order rabbokki, F#139) and the best single content idea ('What the regulars order' as a menu sub-head). But as a world it is the weakest: 'the website is the menu board' is a restaurant-site convention, Archivo Black over Archivo over Space Mono is a stack you can find on a hundred cafe templates, the Laminate plate at #F2E9D8 is the cream the brief tells us to avoid (the direction concedes this in its own risks), the back-eased rubber stamp (cubic-bezier .34,1.56) is the most AI-default motion in any of the three decks, and a fixed Call/Book bar is sensible plumbing, not design. The menu as hero collides with the owner gate: every price on the climactic sheet is an em-dash and a chip, so the 'that's us' beat is a menu full of dashes. Fit is middling because it treats the rooms, which the brief says are the reason the site exists, as one green plate among others. Buildability is excellent: everything is clip-path, a scale and a few circles.

- Biggest risk: It looks like a nice restaurant site. Nothing on the page says 2 AM, karaoke or Kakaʻako once you scroll past the pitcher; the owner has seen this layout on his competitors' Squarespace pages.
- Must graft: The 'What the regulars order: spicy squid, rabbokki' sub-head on the menu (F#139), set as its own row group, and the romanization + one-line gloss under each Hangul word so both ordering cultures read the same page.

## judge:owner

**Verdict:** Backlit wins on this lens. It is the only direction that puts all four counter-moment answers in the first viewport of a 375px phone: the bar's name in Korean above DUCK BUTT in sign red, 5 PM – 2 AM in the same letters, 901 Kawaiahaʻo off Ward, and BOOK A ROOM; and its world is both the street he arrives on and the rooms in back, which is why it fits this specific bar better than the beautiful but narrower NOW PLAYING. NOW PLAYING has the single most electric three seconds (the lyric sweep) and the single best booking idea (the wall phone with his number on the handset), and both must be grafted in; Anju Board loses on distinctiveness but its fixed Call / Book bar is the one piece of pure phone pragmatism none of the others matched and should ship too. Two fixes before building Backlit: switch the hero Hangul to 오리궁뎅이 so it matches the 'oh ri goong deng ee' the owners actually say (F#147), and treat the SVG sign box as a placeholder honest enough that the owner's own comic-duck sign photo (F#162) replaces it the night he hands it over.

### Backlit: the lit box on Kawaiahaʻo — D8 F9 P8 B7 = 32

First three seconds: black, a sign box warms up, 오리궁둥이 above DUCK BUTT in hof red. The owner has stood under a sign exactly like this on Kawaiahaʻo for sixteen years; the hof-sign vocabulary (flat red/green/vinyl, backlit box, roll-up door) is the real material of the block, not a neon cliche. Ten-second test is the best of the three: hours in 40px Anton, the address, and BOOK A ROOM / MENU sit directly under the sign in the first viewport, and FIND US closes with a Ward/Kawaiahaʻo schematic and an 영업중 OPEN chip. 'That's us' lands on the first thumb-scroll with A STRANGE BIRD and the suppliers giggling at the name. Fit is the strongest because it honors both doors: the street and the back rooms. Buildability is fine but not trivial: sign-box gradients, the perforated ticket mask, a marquee strip that verify.js must police, a red that is only legal above 40px, and the Hangul spelling 궁둥이 does not match the quoted 'goong deng ee' pronunciation (F#147), which the owner will notice.

- Biggest risk: The sign is a guess at his sign. F#162 says there is a comic duck on the real one that nobody has seen; if the SVG stand-in looks wrong to him, the first three seconds become 'that is not our sign' instead of 'that is us', and the ballast warm-up on slow cell service can read as a broken page before JS lands.
- Must graft: Hours, address and the BOOK A ROOM / MENU sticker buttons set in the same sign letters inside the first viewport, plus the 영업중 OPEN chip and the not-to-scale Ward-crossing-Kawaiahaʻo strip with an arrow to Blaisdell in Find Us.

### NOW PLAYING: ROOM 2 — D9 F8 P9 B5 = 31

The lyric sweep is the single best three-second moment in the set: DUCK BUTT filling yellow left to right the way the words fill on his own machines every night. No stranger designs that; it is the back room from the owner's side of the counter. The wall phone with his number on the handset as the booking CTA is the smartest answer to 'how do I book a room' in any direction, and the song-book menu with Famous Duck Butt Chicken at 0101 is charming. Ten-second test is weaker: hours are tucked into a body sentence under the hero, the only big 'hours' moment is a flip-digit gimmick in the last section, and the NOW PLAYING chip top-left risks reading as a media player on a phone. Fit is high but narrower than Backlit: it is all back room, no street, no 'off Ward' arrival. Buildability is the lowest: background-clip two-tone sweep, cord draw, flip digits, screen glow and scanlines, a queue nav, all on a dark page the brief says must be read under bar light. It also asserts 'two rooms' from F#162/F#213 while the fact pack's dead-ends say the count is unconfirmed, so that claim needs the gate the other directions already apply.

- Biggest risk: Pastiche and fatigue on a phone: a yellow lyric sweep, screens with scanlines, song codes and a queue nav can tip from 'his room' to 'karaoke theme park', and under a 50%-brightness phone at the bar an oxblood-black page with a few yellow chips is harder to scan than either rival.
- Must graft: The Wall Phone as the booking CTA: a coiled-cord SVG with (808) 593-1880 set on the handset as text, 'PICK UP THE PHONE' as the rooms headline, and the 5–8 / 8–11 block chips beside it. Secondary: the one-time lyric sweep on DUCK BUTT if the hero stays a sign box.

### Anju Board — D5 F7 P6 B9 = 27

The most practical and the easiest to ship: a laminate sheet in a bottle-green frame, Archivo Black, mono prices, a sweating Hite pitcher, and a fixed 48px Call / Book bar on mobile, which is the best ten-second answer in the set because the two things a customer needs are on screen the whole time. The menu-board-as-hero is the right 'that's us' for a bar that won Bronze for Best Bar Food, and 'What the regulars order: spicy squid, rabbokki' is the line that tells him the author understands his two crowds. But the first three seconds are a wall of Archivo Black type on tungsten black with hours as a 12px label, and the laminate-cream sheet framed in green drifts straight toward the AI-default cream page the brief bans; the direction admits this in its own risks. On a phone at 9 p.m. it reads as a well-set restaurant site, not as this bar. Distinctiveness is the lowest because the devices (stamp, pitcher, dotted leaders) are generic diner vocabulary; nothing here is specifically Korean-hof or karaoke-room except the three Hangul tags.

- Biggest risk: It looks like a nice menu site for any bar. The owner will nod, but there is no moment where he reads his own bar's name in Korean before English or sees his own room, so the 'reclaim your web presence tonight' impulse is weaker than the other two.
- Must graft: The fixed 48px Call / Book bottom bar on mobile (phone as text until the tel: flag is verified), and the 'What the regulars order' sub-row that pairs the local order with the Korean-regular order on the menu board.

## judge:build

**Verdict:** Backlit wins, and not narrowly. Through the buildability lens it is the direction that loses the least: the sign box is box-shadow and gradients, the ticket is one mask line, the motions are opacity, clip-path and transform with the lit state in static HTML, and every reading pair recomputes above 9:1 with the red confined to display size. It is also the only direction whose hero is the real place (the gray box, the lit sign, the name in the language the owners say it in), which is what makes the phone demo land in three seconds. Three fixes before it enters the design bible: put the green rail's text in Ink or at large-text size (Vinyl on green is 3.07:1), replace the #B5121B small-red fallback on dark ground with Sodium or Vinyl (it is 2.76:1 on Asphalt), and give the street strip a pause control or render it static on touch devices. Graft Room 2's wall phone as the booking CTA and Anju's stamp-style 'ask' chip for gated prices; both are small SVG/CSS and both strengthen the two things the brief says the site exists for, booking a room and not inventing a price. Room 2 is the runner-up on ideas but ships with two failing palette pairs, the heaviest animation in the set, and a block-time leak; Anju Board builds easiest but puts an all-dashes menu on a cream sheet behind a pinned bar the brief forbids.

### Backlit: the lit box on Kawaiahaʻo — D9 F9 P9 B8 = 35

Every stated ratio checks out when recomputed (Vinyl/Asphalt 16.75, Ink/Vinyl 16.41, VinylDim/Asphalt 10.66, Sodium/Asphalt 9.16, Ink/Sodium 8.98, Watermelon/Asphalt 5.94, SignRed/Vinyl 4.22 large-only). The build is almost entirely box-shadow, gradients, a clip-path reveal, a perforation mask and three small SVGs; the motion set is opacity/transform/clip-path only, every pre-state lives under html.js, and the hero is lit in static HTML so a JS failure or slow cell load shows a finished sign, not a black page. The sign-box container system doubles as the photo-slot frame, so the owner's real sign photo drops in without redesign. It is the only direction whose hero is the actual building, and 오리궁둥이 above DUCK BUTT at 24vw is the strongest three-second phone moment of the three. Deductions: four font families (Archivo with a width axis is the heavy one; subset to Latin and ship wdth 75/100 only), and the spec contradicts itself twice on contrast, listed under risk.

- Biggest risk: Three AA leaks in the spec as written: (1) the hero's green rail carries text (CAFÉ · KOREAN TAPAS · SOJU · KARAOKE ROOMS) while the palette rules say green never carries text; Vinyl on Bottle Green is 3.07:1, so that rail must be Ink on green (5.35:1) or the text must be ≥24px/18.66px bold. (2) The stated small-red fallback #B5121B is 6.06:1 on Vinyl but only 2.76:1 on Asphalt, a hard fail; on the dark ground small red must become Sodium or Vinyl, never a darker red. (3) Sign Red on Asphalt is 3.97:1, unlisted, so the 40px floor must apply on the dark ground too. Also the 40s infinite marquee has no pause affordance on touch (hover/focus do not exist on a phone); WCAG 2.2.2 needs a visible pause button or a static wrapped line on coarse pointers.
- Must graft: The sign-box container with Hangul rails over English headers (안주 / THE BOARD, 노래방 / THE ROOMS, 영업중 on the OPEN chip) as the section-marking system, and the perforated Space Mono ticket whose gated values print an em-dash plus a chip, which is the cleanest answer to the fact-gating rule.

### NOW PLAYING: ROOM 2 — D8 F7 P8 B6 = 29

The core pairs pass with margin (LyricWhite/RoomBlack 16.76, LyricWhite/Vinyl 13.21, Ink/CueYellow 13.61, ScreenBlue/RoomBlack 9.33, Watermelon/RoomBlack 6.02). The wall phone, song-book codes and lyric sweep are specific to this room and nobody else's, and the queue nav that wraps at 375 is well thought through. But the two most expensive effects in all three directions are here: the lyric sweep animates the split point of a background-clip:text gradient on an 84 to 160px glyph (not compositable; full repaint every frame for 1.8s on load, exactly when a cheap phone is also parsing fonts), and the Screen-on uses an animated filter: brightness on a glowing panel. Both are once-only and have reduced-motion fallbacks, so they ship, but they are the jank risk at the counter. Black Han Sans as the only Latin display face is a taste risk at 160px: its Latin set is serviceable, not handsome. The lyric-highlight and scanline vocabulary also drifts toward the retro-CRT look the brief warns about.

- Biggest risk: Palette pairs that fail: Smoke (#8E8389) on Vinyl (#3B1C23) is 4.18:1 and on Lyric White paper is 3.16:1, so Smoke meta text is only legal on Room Black and the #0B0809 Screen panel; the romanization under the hero and the quote sources are fine, but any Smoke caption on the What-this-is or Rooms panels or the song-book margin note fails AA at 12px. ScreenBlue on paper is 1.80:1, so focus rings over the song book need a dark ring. Fact gating also leaks: the spec says the 3-hour blocks are single-source and should read 'ask about blocks', then renders 17:00–20:00 and 20:00–23:00 as queue chips, and the counter moment leans on 'the 8–11 block'. The watermelon bleeding off the right edge at 375 is the classic horizontal-overflow trap and needs an overflow-clipped parent.
- Must graft: The Wall Phone SVG as the booking CTA with (808) 593-1880 set on the handset as text, cord drawn with pathLength=1 and the tel: link gated behind the verified flag. It is the single best 'that's us' object in any direction and it is 160×220 of trivial SVG.

### Anju Board — D5 F7 P5 B8 = 25

The simplest build of the three and the most conservative contrast table; every pair recomputes at or above the claim (Chilled/Ink 17.92, Ink/Laminate 15.79, Laminate/Rind 10.43, Tungsten/Ink 10.79, Glass/Ink 12.37, Tungsten/Rind 7.13), and Gochujang/Laminate is actually 4.73:1, which passes normal text, not just large. Motion is transform/opacity plus one clip-path reveal. Four families under 120KB is realistic. The problem is the concept under the constraints: the page's declared hero is a laminated menu board, and every price on it is gated to an em-dash. A menu whose entire price column reads '—' is a hero that looks unfinished no matter how good the stamp is. Laminate #F2E9D8 is the cream the brief names as an AI default, and the spec concedes it. Tungsten amber plus cream plus sauce red reads as generic warm-restaurant, not Kakaʻako at 1 a.m.

- Biggest risk: It violates constraint 6 outright: 'the only fixed element is a 48px bottom bar with Call and Book' is a pinned element on mobile, it eats 48px of a 667px viewport, collides with iOS Safari's toolbar and safe-area insets, and covers the footer credit; it has to be dropped or become an in-flow block. The Song Queue rows ('Room 1 · 8–11 PM') print both an unconfirmed single-source block time and an implied room count that the facts contradict (F#6 'several' vs F#162/F#213 'two'). The Pitcher bleeding off the right edge is a 375px overflow risk, and its 2400ms queue tick is the most distracting idle animation of the three.
- Must graft: The 'ask' chip designed as a rotated Gochujang-style stamp so gated em-dashes read as deliberate, and the menu sub-head 'What the regulars order: spicy squid, rabbokki' (F#139), which is the best single use of a fact in any direction.
