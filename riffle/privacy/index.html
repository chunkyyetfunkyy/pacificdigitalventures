Riffle Privacy Policy

Effective October 25, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026 for the duplicate finder and the Home Screen widget

The short version

Riffle has no account, no server, no analytics and no ads. Your photos, and every keep-or-bin decision you make, stay on your iPhone. We never receive them, and we never receive anything that identifies you. The only things that leave your iPhone go to Apple, for features built into iOS: place names and maps, your own iCloud Photos, and App Store purchases.

1. Who we are

Riffle is made by Pacific Digital Ventures LLC of Honolulu, Hawaiʻi, USA (“we” or “us”). If you have a question about this policy, email info@pacificdigitalventures.org.

2. What Riffle uses on your iPhone

Your photo library

Riffle asks for access to your photos the first time you open it. You can give it your whole library or only the photos you pick, and change that at any time in the Settings app under Riffle › Photos (Apps › Riffle › Photos in iOS 18 and later). With access, Riffle reads:

  • your photos and videos, to show them as cards and full screen, and to compare photos taken within two minutes of each other for the duplicate finder;
  • their details: the date taken, the location if the photo has one, the file size, whether it’s a favorite, and which photos were taken together.

Riffle changes your library only when you ask it to:

  • Emptying the bin deletes the photos in it, after iOS asks you to confirm. They move to the Recently Deleted album in Photos, where you can recover them for 30 days. If iCloud Photos is on, they are deleted from every device signed in to your iCloud account.
  • Tapping the heart marks or unmarks a photo as a favorite.

Notifications (optional)

If you turn on the reminder, Riffle schedules one notification for each bin you empty, on the day its 30 days in Recently Deleted are up. The reminder is scheduled on your iPhone; no server is involved.

3. What Riffle keeps, and where

Everything Riffle remembers is stored on your iPhone, in Riffle’s own app storage:

  • your review history: which photos you kept or binned, when, and how big they were. Photos are recorded by the photo library’s internal identifier, not copied;
  • what’s waiting in the bin, and totals of the space you’ve cleared;
  • your settings, such as deck size and filters;
  • your iCloud plan and storage used, only if you choose to enter them. Riffle can’t read these from iOS;
  • for the duplicate finder, a fingerprint of each photo taken close to another, a score for how good a shot it is, and its file size, all worked out on your iPhone by Apple’s Vision framework and Photos. A fingerprint is a list of numbers describing what the photo looks like; it isn’t the photo and can’t be turned back into it;
  • if you add the Home Screen widget, small copies of up to eight unreviewed photos, with their dates and place names, in a folder on your iPhone that only Riffle and its widget can read. Screenshots, favorites and photos that are mostly text are never used: Riffle checks each photo for text on your iPhone first. Keep and Bin taps in the widget wait in the same folder until you next open Riffle. Once you remove the widget, the copies are deleted the next time you open Riffle.

None of this is sent to us or to anyone else. Like other app data, it can be included in your iPhone’s own backups (iCloud Backup or a computer), which you control.

4. What leaves your iPhone, and only to Apple

Riffle has no servers of its own. A few features use Apple services built into iOS:

  • Place names. For a photo with a location, Riffle sends its coordinates, not the photo, to Apple’s location service to get a place name such as “Honolulu, United States”. Riffle keeps the answer only while the app is running.
  • Maps. The details sheet shows an Apple Maps view of where a photo was taken, which loads map images of that area from Apple.
  • iCloud Photos. If your photos are stored in iCloud, iOS downloads the full-size photo or video from your iCloud account when Riffle shows or plays it. Riffle never sees your Apple Account.
  • Purchases. Riffle Pro is bought and restored through the App Store. Apple handles payment, so we never see your name, card or Apple Account; the app only learns whether Pro is unlocked. We receive Apple’s standard sales reports, which don’t identify you.
  • Ratings. Riffle may show Apple’s standard rating prompt, at most once. Any rating or review you give goes to Apple.
  • Crash reports. If you’ve turned on Share With App Developers in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements, Apple may share crash reports and app usage statistics with us. That’s Apple’s setting, and it works the same way for every app.

Apple’s handling of this information is covered by Apple’s Privacy Policy.

5. What we don’t do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, advertising or tracking code, and no third-party SDKs.
  • No tracking you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • No uploading your photos anywhere.
  • No selling, renting or sharing your information. We don’t have it.

6. Your choices

  • Photo access: in the Settings app under Riffle › Photos, choose which photos Riffle can see, or turn access off.
  • Forget your history: Riffle’s Settings › Forget review history erases every keep and bin decision. Your total of space cleared is kept.
  • Reminders: turn them off in Riffle’s Settings, or in Settings › Notifications › Riffle.
  • The widget: remove it from your Home Screen, and its photo copies are deleted the next time you open Riffle.
  • Delete everything: deleting Riffle from your iPhone deletes everything it stored. Photos in Riffle’s bin that you haven’t emptied are left untouched in your library.

7. Children

Riffle isn’t directed at children under 13, and it collects no personal information from anyone, children included.

8. Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, for example under the GDPR in the EU and UK or the CCPA in California, you may have the right to see, correct or delete the personal information a company holds about you. We don’t hold any personal information about Riffle users, so there is nothing for us to hand over or delete. What Riffle stores is on your iPhone and under your control, as described above. If you have questions, email us.

9. Security

Riffle’s data lives in its private app storage on your iPhone, which iOS keeps apart from other apps and encrypts with the rest of your device. Connections to Apple’s services are encrypted by iOS.

10. Changes to this policy

If Riffle ever changes what it does with data, for example a new feature that needs to send something off your iPhone, we’ll update this page and Riffle’s App Store privacy details before that version is released, and change the date at the top.
