Deleting and getting photos back

When does Riffle actually delete a photo?

Only when you empty the bin. Swiping left puts a photo in Riffle’s bin, where nothing has left your library yet. Tapping Empty bin asks iOS to delete everything in it, and iOS shows its own confirmation first. Deleted photos go to the Recently Deleted album in Photos, where they stay for 30 days.

If iCloud Photos is on, deleting removes the photo from every device signed in to your iCloud account.

I binned a photo by mistake. How do I get it back?

If the bin hasn’t been emptied, open the bin and tap the photo to rescue it, or tap Rescue all. It goes back to unreviewed, so Riffle can deal it again.

If you already emptied the bin, open the Photos app and go to Albums › Recently Deleted. Select the photo and tap Recover. This works for 30 days after deleting.

I emptied the bin. Why hasn’t my iCloud storage gone down?

iOS keeps deleted photos in Recently Deleted for 30 days, and they still count toward your storage until then. Riffle shows these batches under Clearing in the bin, with the day each one lands. To free the space now, open Photos, go to Albums › Recently Deleted, and tap Delete All. That can’t be undone.

Using Riffle

Riffle only shows some of my photos.

You probably gave Riffle access to selected photos only. To let it deal from your whole library, open the Settings app, go to Riffle › Photos (Apps › Riffle › Photos on iOS 18 and later), and choose Full Access.

Why does Riffle show dollar amounts for some people and not others?

Riffle can’t read your iCloud plan or how much storage you use. It shows money only after you enter your plan, under Settings › Storage & savings in Riffle. Adding your iCloud usage (shown in the Settings app under your name › iCloud) makes the numbers exact. Prices are US iCloud+ rates.

How does the duplicate finder decide what’s a duplicate?

It compares photos taken within two minutes of each other, on your iPhone, and groups the ones that look nearly the same. The best shot of each set is marked BEST, chosen by sharpness, exposure and open eyes, with favorites and edited photos first.

  • Only near-identical extras are ticked for you; shots that differ a little more are shown but left to you.
  • Favorites and edited photos are never ticked for you.
  • Binning from a set only puts photos in Riffle’s bin, like a swipe.
  • The first scan of a big library takes a minute or two; later scans only check new photos.
How do I add the Home Screen widget?

Touch and hold an empty spot on your Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and search for Riffle. It comes in small and medium sizes. Open Riffle once afterwards so it can fill the widget with photos.

Keep and Bin on the widget are part of Riffle Pro. Taps made there are added to Riffle the next time you open it. Screenshots, favorites and photos that are mostly text are never shown on the widget.

How do I start over?

In Riffle, open Settings › Forget history and tap Forget review history. Every photo can be dealt again; your total of space cleared is kept. Photos waiting in the bin are untouched.

Riffle Pro

What does Pro include, and what stays free?

Swiping, the bin, Rewind, the details sheet and full-screen viewing are free, with no limits. Pro adds Heavy Hitters (your biggest files first), the duplicate finder’s binning beyond the first three sets, Keep and Bin on the widget, endless decks, and year and album filters.

Pro is $19.99 once (Lifetime) or $14.99 a year (Yearly), in US dollars; the App Store shows your local price.

I bought Pro, but Riffle doesn’t show it.

Make sure you’re signed in with the same Apple Account you bought it with. Then, in Riffle, open Settings › Pro and tap Restore purchases, or tap Restore at the bottom of the Pro screen.

How do I cancel Yearly, or ask for a refund?

Apple handles billing, so both happen with Apple. To cancel, open the Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions › Riffle. To ask for a refund, go to reportaproblem.apple.com. Lifetime is a single purchase with nothing to cancel.

Privacy

Riffle has no account, server or analytics, and your photos stay on your iPhone. The Riffle Privacy Policy has the details.
